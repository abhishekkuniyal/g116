1. Introduction

Fertilizer Demand & Agricultural Logistics Ecosystem

Critical role of balanced fertilization and IFFCO's national distribution footprint
Urea (46% Nitrogen) — The High-Volume Engine
  • Dominant Consumption: Accounts for ~59% of India's total fertilizer consumption (387+ Lakh MT projected for 2025-26).
  • Agronomic Role: Essential for vegetative canopy growth and chlorophyll synthesis in paddy, wheat, and sugarcane.
  • Price Distortion: Heavily subsidized at statutory controlled rates, making its state-level demand highly elastic to acreage.
DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate) — Basal Rooting Nutrient
  • Composition: 18% Nitrogen and 46% Phosphorus; critical basal dressing applied directly at sowing time.
  • Import Vulnerability: High dependence on imported rock phosphate and phosphoric acid; subject to global price swings.
  • Projected Demand: 96.27 Lakh MT projected for 2025-26 with peak concentration in northern and central grain belts.
NPKS Complexes — Balanced Soil Health Nutrition
  • Multi-Nutrient Balance: Tailored ratios (e.g. 10:26:26, 12:32:16, 20:20:0:13) designed for specific agro-climatic zones.
  • Rapid Adoption: Growing at over 6% annually as government policies encourage balanced NPK ratios over pure urea.
  • Projected Demand: 149.72 Lakh MT for 2025-26, representing the second largest fertilizer category in India.
MOP (Muriate of Potash) — Stress Tolerance & Quality
  • 100% Import Dependent: India has zero domestic potash reserves; entire supply relies on imports from Canada, Russia, and Jordan.
  • Agronomic Role: Regulates plant water balance, enzyme activation, and disease resistance in cash crops (cotton, fruits, oilseeds).
  • Projected Demand: 22.01 Lakh MT for 2025-26; low absolute volume but crucial for balanced yield.

2. Motivation

Supply Chain Bottlenecks & Fiscal Vulnerabilities

Operational friction points and high costs of misallocated fertilizer stocks
Crop Loss from Sowing Delays
  • Strict Sowing Windows: In rain-fed Kharif agriculture, sowing must coincide with monsoon arrival within a 10-14 day window.
  • Yield Degradation: A 7 to 10 day delay in basal fertilizer application results in up to 15–20% permanent crop yield loss.
  • Farmer Panic & Hoarding: Localized perceived deficits trigger panic purchases, exacerbating artificial stockouts.
Multi-Modal Transit Lead Times
  • Port to Field Delay: Raw materials and imported finished goods require 21 to 30 days from port offloading to rake dispatch.
  • Railway Rake Bottlenecks: Dedicated freight corridors face seasonal rake shortages during harvest cycles.
  • Demurrage Penalties: Miscalculated destination quotas cause railway rake congestion, incurring millions in demurrage fees.
Subsidy Fiscal Burden (₹1.6L Cr)
  • Massive National Outlay: The Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers disburses ₹1.6+ Lakh Crore annually in fertilizer subsidies.
  • Working Capital Drag: Excess buffer stock sitting in regional godowns traps cooperative capital and risks bag caking.
  • Audit Compliance: Requires defensible, transparent forecasting methodologies for parliamentary and CAG scrutiny.
The Core Engineering Challenge

Develop an automated, mathematically rigorous demand forecasting system that reliably predicts state-level fertilizer quotas 12 months ahead, while insulating supply planners against regional data anomalies and overfitted machine learning models.

4. Gap Analysis

Limitations of Prior Work & Project G116 Solutions

Bridging academic machine learning research with industrial deployment realities
Gap 1: Over-Complexity in Annual Macro Series
  • Existing Literature Flaw: Many academic studies claim high accuracy with deep neural nets or complex ensembles without benchmarking against rigorous persistence baselines.
  • G116 Resolution: Executed an exhaustive 5-year rolling evaluation proving that Naive Persistence beats Random Forest in 4 out of 5 years (MAE 0.4349 vs 0.5203).
Gap 2: Neglecting Administrative Data Breaks
  • Existing Literature Flaw: Automated ML pipelines blindly fit to sudden statistical jumps without investigating whether they represent real market demand or reporting artifacts.
  • G116 Resolution: Uncovered the 2018-19 UP/Uttarakhand data swap; preserved data provenance while engineering a `potential_reporting_break` binary safeguard.
Gap 3: Spatial & Temporal Grain Mismatches
  • Existing Literature Flaw: Incompatible datasets (e.g. district supply logs with state-level records) are often haphazardly merged, distorting national totals.
  • G116 Resolution: Established a strict integration contract isolating the auxiliary Rajasthan district dataset from the primary 36-state national forecasting model.
Gap 4: Disconnect from Production Software
  • Existing Literature Flaw: Most published research terminates in un-reproducible Jupyter notebooks with zero API infrastructure or end-user decision tools.
  • G116 Resolution: Engineered an enterprise 4-tier stack: Python Pipeline ➔ Microsoft SQL Server ➔ FastAPI REST Service ➔ Modern React Analytics UI.
5. Problem Formulation

Mathematical Formulation & Core Project Objectives

Rigorous temporal boundary conditions, objective functions, and target metrics
Formal Mathematical Formulation

Let i ∈ {1 ... 36} index States and UTs, j ∈ {Urea, DAP, MOP, NPKS} index chemical fertilizers, and t ∈ [2014-15 ... 2024-25] index Financial Years.

Target: Yi, j, t+1 = F( Xi, j, ≤ t ) + εi, j, t+1
  • Xi, j, t: Feature vector containing sales(t), sales(t-1), requirement(t), requirement(t-1), availability(t), availability(t-1), and plant indicators.
  • Zero Lookahead Bias: Strict temporal boundary ensures information available at time t is never contaminated by future t+1 data.
  • Optimization Objective: Minimize Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) across all 144 target series.
Key Project Objectives
  • Obj 1: Multi-Model Benchmark: Evaluate Naive Persistence, Linear Regression, Random Forest, Random Forest (+Features), Drift, and Recent-Change baselines.
  • Obj 2: Expanding Rolling Cross-Validation: Test models across 5 successive fiscal forecast periods (2019-20 to 2023-24) totaling 720 out-of-sample predictions.
  • Obj 3: 2025-26 Production Forecast: Generate verified demand predictions for all 144 state-fertilizer series with 0 missing and 0 negative values.
  • Obj 4: Production Enterprise Platform: Implement an asynchronous FastAPI REST service and interactive React analytics dashboard.
6. Proposed Methodology

Multi-Source Data Ingestion & Engineering Pipeline

Schema harmonization, feature engineering, and chronological data partitioning
Stage 1: Multi-Source Heterogeneous Data Ingestion
Government MCF Dataset

11 financial years (2014-15 to 2024-25) across 36 States/UTs capturing Requirement, Availability, and Sales in LMT for 4 fertilizer classes.

IFFCO Plant Production Logs

Plant-level manufacturing records from Kalol, Kandla, Phulpur, Paradeep, and Aonla aggregated by state and financial year.

Rajasthan District Supply

District-level supply logs maintained as an auxiliary satellite store to prevent data grain distortion.

Stage 2: Entity Harmonization & Provenance Integrity
  • Spelling Normalization: Standardized geographic names (e.g. 'Orissa' ➔ 'Odisha', 'Pondicherry' ➔ 'Puducherry', 'Jammu and Kashmir' ➔ 'Jammu & Kashmir').
  • Aggregate Isolation: Preserved the 'All India' aggregate records in an isolated table to prevent double-counting state series.
  • Missing Value Semantics: Treated missing IFFCO plant production as 'unmonitored or no manufacturing plant', NOT as zero sales in that state.
Stage 3: Feature Store Construction (1,440 Rows × 14 Columns)
  • Temporal Lag Features: `sales_lag_1`, `requirement_lag_1`, `availability_lag_1`.
  • Derived Indicators: `iffco_production_available` (binary), `potential_reporting_break` (binary).
  • Strict Chronological Sorting: Enforced ordering by financial year, state, and fertilizer type prior to feature generation.

7. Data Quality Discovery

The 2018-19 Reporting Discontinuity in UP & Uttarakhand

Empirical detection, impact analysis, and engineering safeguard protocol
Anomalous Drop in Uttar Pradesh (FY 2018-19)

In FY 2018-19, Uttar Pradesh recorded an unprecedented statistical collapse in official sales records:

  • DAP Sales Plunge: Dropped from 18.08 LMT in 2017-18 to 0.22 LMT in 2018-19 (a 98.8% decline).
  • Urea Sales Plunge: Dropped from 58.92 LMT in 2017-18 to 2.75 LMT in 2018-19 (a 95.3% decline).
  • In real agricultural consumption, such a collapse is physically impossible without catastrophic famine, pointing directly to a reporting reallocation.
Simultaneous Surge in Uttarakhand (FY 2018-19)

Coinciding with the UP drop, neighboring Uttarakhand recorded an unnatural multi-thousand percent surge:

  • DAP Sales Surge: Surged from 0.29 LMT in 2017-18 to 17.17 LMT in 2018-19 (a 5,800% increase!).
  • Urea Sales Surge: Surged from 3.10 LMT in 2017-18 to 62.86 LMT in 2018-19 (a 1,900% increase!).
  • These numbers represent an exact numerical swap with Uttar Pradesh's typical volumes, reflecting an administrative reporting change.
G116 Data Provenance & Safeguard Protocol
  • Provenance Rule: Never overwrite or 'correct' official government numbers without official administrative gazette documentation.
  • Automated Flag: Engineered the `potential_reporting_break` binary column to isolate these 4 series during model evaluation.
  • Decision Recommendation: Flagged in executive dashboards so supply chain planners interpret Uttarakhand quotas with appropriate context.

8. Machine Learning Suite

Multi-Model Architecture & Baseline Benchmark Suite

Exhaustive evaluation of 6 distinct statistical and machine learning approaches
1. Naive Persistence

Equation: F(t+1) = Sales(t)

  • Selected production model.
  • Leverages massive autoregressive inertia in annual agricultural quotas.
  • Zero parameter estimation risk; lowest rolling error.
2. Linear Regression

Architecture: One-Hot Encoding + OLS

  • Estimates linear trend slopes and product categorical offsets.
  • Highly vulnerable to multicollinearity between requirement & availability.
  • Suffered high rolling RMSE.
3. Random Forest (Base)

Architecture: 200 Trees, max_features='sqrt'

  • Captures non-linear feature splits across state volumes.
  • Retained as primary machine learning benchmark.
  • Won over Naive persistence in 2023-24.
4. RF (+Features)

Features: Δ Sales, Requirement Gap

  • Engineered acceleration and supply-gap ratios.
  • Effective for identifying momentum shifts.
  • Marginal gains over standard Random Forest.
5. Drift Baseline

Equation: F(t+1) = y_t + (y_t - y_0)/t

  • Extrapolates average historical growth rate from initial observation.
  • Prone to overshooting if early years had low bases.
6. Recent-Change Baseline

Equation: F(t+1) = y_t + (y_t - y_{t-1})

  • Pure momentum extrapolation.
  • Severe error inflation during mean-reverting seasons.

Key Empirical Findings
  • Why Naive Persistence Wins: Annual state demand is strongly anchored by agricultural acreage, crop patterns, and institutional quotas, creating heavy autocorrelation.

15. Limitations

Operational Constraints & Responsible Deployment Guidelines

Boundary conditions and deployment guardrails for agricultural planners
1. Annual Granularity Limitation
  • Constraint: The model operates on an annual financial-year granularity.
  • Operational Rule: It must NOT be misrepresented as a monthly or weekly scheduling system. Rail rake scheduling requires secondary downscaling using seasonal Kharif/Rabi ratios.
2. Auxiliary Data Grain Isolation
  • Constraint: IFFCO Rajasthan data is district-level without chemical type segregation.
  • Operational Rule: Direct merging into the 36-state national forecasting dataset was intentionally avoided to prevent aggregation artifacts.
3. Plant Production Data Semantics
  • Constraint: Plant manufacturing records are only available for states hosting IFFCO factories.
  • Operational Rule: Missing plant records signify unmonitored or non-manufacturing states, NOT zero consumption.
4. Nature of 2025-26 Predictions
  • Constraint: All 2025-26 values are algorithmic estimates based on FY 2024-25 baseline sales.
  • Operational Rule: They represent expected demand under normal agricultural weather conditions and should be updated if extreme monsoon anomalies occur.

Conclusion & Future Research Roadmap

Summary of academic contributions and strategic technical enhancements
Key Project Contributions
  • Empirical Rigor: Proved that on small annual macroeconomic series (N=11), Naive Persistence achieves superior stability (MAE 0.4349) compared to overparameterized ML models.
  • Hybrid Retainment: Retained Random Forest as a critical secondary benchmark, validating its advantage during structural transition years (2023-24).

Future Research Roadmap

  • Weather & Monsoon Indices: Incorporate IMD rainfall deviation indices and reservoir water levels as dynamic features.
  • Railway Rake Dispatch Optimization: Link demand forecasts directly with Indian Railways freight scheduling systems.
